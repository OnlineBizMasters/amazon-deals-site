How to calculate a commercial cleaning bid

A reliable janitorial bid should start with the labor required to service the facility, then add direct costs, overhead and the margin your company needs. Square-foot pricing can be useful as a final comparison, but using square footage alone can hide labor-heavy jobs.

What this calculator does differently

  • Uses your own production rate instead of assuming a universal cleaning rate.
  • Separates base wage from payroll burden so labor is not understated.
  • Calculates margin correctly instead of confusing margin with markup.
  • Shows monthly and annual contract values for recurring janitorial work.
  • Shows price per square foot per visit as a sanity-check metric.

Commercial cleaning bid calculator vs. pricing per square foot

Price per square foot is useful for checking whether a bid looks reasonable, but facilities with the same area can require very different labor. Restrooms, break rooms, floor type, density, security requirements and service frequency can materially change the time required. For that reason, this calculator treats production rate and labor cost as primary inputs.

Frequently asked questions

Is this commercial cleaning bid calculator free?

Yes. The calculator is free to use and does not require an account.

Does it use a fixed cleaning rate per square foot?

No. You enter your own production rate and costs. This avoids presenting an unverified industry rate as universally correct.

What is payroll burden?

Payroll burden is the employer cost above base wage, such as payroll taxes, workers compensation and benefits. Use your own actual percentage.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a percentage of selling price. Markup is profit added as a percentage of cost. This calculator prices from your target margin.

Can I use this for office cleaning bids?

Yes, as long as you enter inputs that reflect the actual office and your own operating costs.
